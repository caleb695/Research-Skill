# Fixed task prompt — authoritative

This is the complete task this skill runs. It is not a template and it is not
replaced by command arguments. Read it before the first round and treat it as the
user's task statement for every round, resume, and final report.

```text
I want you to indefinitely research online for evidence for and against the Book of Mormon being true.

Look for this nhs online like archeological evidence, bible evidence, accounts, etc.

In each piece of evidence you find I want you to

1. Check to make sure this piece of evidence is real and not just made up (ex: if someone gave misinformation).
2. Look at the context of the situation to understand the POV and all the facts (ex: a verse from the bible but a couple verses later it adds on to that verse)
3. Decide how credible, relevant, and useful this evidence is (ex: does this really provide more evidence for or against it or is it something that could be not true or faked)
4. Generally research the fact extensively to understand everything about it.

Note that people can lie and people can be wrong so don’t take everything at face value. Also note that not every one piece of evidence is equal to another piece of evidence (ex: just because there are several pieces of evidence supporting one claim, one piece of evidence could be worth more on the other side. Rank each piece of evidence you find for how credible, accurate, relevant, and useful the evidence actually is overall (one score).

- Don’t repeat pieces of evidence.
- Use many reliable sources (as many reliable sources as you can find) and do not use not reliable sources such as Wikipedia, Reddit, quora, etc. and try not to use biased sources.
- Once you find evidence for it, look for evidence against it, then back to evidence for it and repeat.
- Create two separate documents, one for it, and one against it.
- Evaluate each piece of evidence against evidence on the other side.
- For evidence use the bible and Book of Mormon (assuming the bible is true), witnesses, archeological evidence, things that wouldn’t make sense if the Book of Mormon isn’t true, the likelihood of something, events from throughout history that are involved with the Mormon church, etc. And use all evidence from throughout history. Even things such as spiritual experiences (but things known historically not like a random unreliable person sharing a story)
- If you can’t find more evidence, go deeper and don’t just rely on things that other people have found, also try to find/uncover things yourself that haven’t been found or considered largely yet.
- If you need to, read the bible and Book of Mormon to search it all for evidence instead of just what people find.
- If you keep searching and have been searching without finding something for a large amount of time, then look back on things you are unsure of or things you are sure of for just more evidence to support a claim.
- When I say stop, stop researching and compile everything you have found for two, overall reports with organized information and claims each with an overall ranking (and one for each category) and evidence for the claim listed each also with an overall ranking and a ranking for each piece of evidence. Until then, keep working.
```

## Operational reading

- The phrase “Look for this nhs online” is preserved verbatim above; operationally
  read it as looking for things online such as archaeological evidence, biblical
  evidence, accounts, and related primary/scholarly sources.
- “Assuming the bible is true” is the user's stipulated premise. Label it in both
  reports; still check text, translation, context, and interpretation.
- The rest of this skill package defines how to execute the fixed prompt:
  alternating for/against rounds, authentication, context/POV, one overall score,
  deduplication, cross-side evaluation, durable ledger, and two ranked reports on
  stop. It does not authorize a different task.
